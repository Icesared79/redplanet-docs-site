# Where We Are Going

Atlas is not a finished product. It is a platform with a defined trajectory toward deeper data, broader verified coverage, more capable intelligence, and more built on top of it.

The quality floor is what makes that trajectory mean anything, because scale without trust is noise.

## Depth Before Breadth

The instinct in this business is to add states. We are doing the opposite first.

Florida is the deepest market and the template, with statewide parcel and transaction coverage, a community layer across **478** jurisdictions, siting and environmental constraints, and a site suitability score that is active there and nowhere else. Finishing that pattern is worth more than starting it in ten more places, because the second state costs a fraction of the first and an unfinished state is worth close to nothing.

The next markets follow the rollout order the data center work sets: North Carolina, then Georgia, Ohio, Texas, and Virginia, with Connecticut as the policy state.

## Closing the Gaps We Have Named

Three limits described elsewhere in this documentation are active work rather than permanent conditions.

**Distress coverage is county-level.** Florida lis pendens reaches 12 of 67 counties, and the Carolinas are metro-concentrated. Every additional county is a separate source with its own system.

**The public testimony layer is a pilot.** Transcripts and recordings cover a small fraction of the meetings we track. What residents say at the podium is often the earliest signal of a decision, so turning that from a pilot into a covered layer is the highest-value expansion in the community layer.

**Cadence is uneven.** Sources run nightly, weekly, quarterly, or on demand, and a number of registered sources have not returned data recently. Bringing the long-cadence lanes to the reliability of the nightly lane is ongoing.

## Intelligence Depth

The signal layer is still early relative to what the underlying asset can support. As the record deepens, the intelligence derivable from it expands more than proportionally, because signals compound across layers.

The direction is entity-level: connecting ownership patterns across markets and asset classes, tying a land assembly to the buyer behind the LLC, and linking what a jurisdiction is about to decide to the parcels it will affect. The value is in the join, and the join only becomes possible once the layers underneath are deep enough to support it.

## Scale

Atlas holds **515,121,435** verified records today, and growth is driven by expanding source coverage rather than by re-counting what is already there.

We are deliberately not publishing a headline record-count target. A record-count milestone is easy to hit by ingesting large, low-value datasets, and optimizing for it would make the asset worse. The figures that matter are verified coverage in named markets and the share of the asset that clears the top quality tier.

## The Platform Ecosystem

Red Planet builds on Atlas today. The roadmap includes third parties and clients building on it directly, through the API, through white-label arrangements, and through licensing. The platform model becomes more valuable as more is built on it and the asset continues to grow underneath.

## The Competitive Position

Legacy providers are expensive, rigid, and slow to change, built on infrastructure and business models designed for a world before autonomous data engineering existed.

The position we hold is narrower and more durable than being cheaper. Three things compound in our favor. The asset is assembled directly rather than licensed, so it cannot be cancelled. Much of what it holds was overwritten at the source and cannot be collected again by anyone at any price. And the development model builds products faster than a traditional organization can decide to.
